                             These results are in agreement with the study of DeSisto et al. [52], who experienced
                          comparable values for CHN and HHV for fast pyrolysis of pine sawdust in a ﬂuidized-bed
                                reactor. Although this solid carbonaceous material is certainly not to be considered the
                        main target when operating fast pyrolysis units, it represents a highly valuable by-product.
                           Moreover, the well-known catalytic effect of char on the volatiles [54] is what motivates the
                             application of a volatile/solid separation device.
                           The elemental composition and properties of char are strictly dependent on the pro-
                                cess’s main parameters, such as temperature and heating rate. Carbon content is expected
                               to increase with temperature while concurrently, oxygen and hydrogen levels decrease [55].
                            Nevertheless, the char considered in this study has been produced at ﬁxed experiment
                             conditions, i.e., Treact = 500 ◦C, since operational variations have been applied only to the
                           condensation train.

                                   3.3. Non-Condensable Gas Analysis

                               outlet after the ESP or after the spray condensers train mainly consisted of N2 (the carrier
                                gas), equal to ≃92% v/v, and a small portion of pyrogas, around 8% v/v, generated during
