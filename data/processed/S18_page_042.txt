Enzymatic hydrolysis and fermentation experiments are typically “integrated” with pretreatment,
meaning that a single large batch of pretreated material is used to perform many hydrolysis and
fermentation experiments. Integrated performance results for the process configuration and
operating conditions assumed in the 2002 design (i.e., 20% total solids loading with overliming)
[2] were first produced in 2005. In 2008, integrated experimental results advanced significantly
toward the 2012 targets following the replacement of overliming with ammonium hydroxide
conditioning, which demonstrated a slightly improved cellulose-to-ethanol yield and
significantly better xylose-to-ethanol yield due to decreased sugar losses and improved
hydrolysate fermentability [32].
