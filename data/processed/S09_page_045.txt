                   Laboratory - Exercise 1

Investigations of Biodiesel Production Pitfalls

Equipment needed:
Four 500 ml Erlenmeyer flasks containing 200 g of soybean oil
Four 125 ml Erlenmeyer flasks, 3 containing 43 g of methanol, one containing 21.5 g of
methanol
Four hot plate/stirrers
Four separatory funnels with ringstands
Aluminum foil (for capping flasks)
Small bottles containing preweighed sodium hydroxide, three with 2 g, one with 10 g.

Group 1: Too much water (How much harm can a little water do?)
   •   Starting with 200 g of soybean oil in a 500 ml flask. Add 10 g of water.
   •  Add 2 g of sodium hydroxide pellets to 43 g of methanol and agitate until the pellets are
       dissolved.
   •  Add the methanol/sodium hydroxide solution to the soybean oil. Add a magnetic stirring
       bar and place on the hot plate. Heat to 60-65ºC and agitate for 1 hour. Pour into a
       separatory funnel and allow to separate.
Does separation occur?
What happens when you add water?

Group 2: Too much catalyst (If a little bit is good, more must be better)
   •   Start with 200 g of soybean oil in a 500 ml flask.
   •  Add 10 g of sodium hydroxide pellets to 43 g of methanol and agitate until the pellets are
       dissolved. Heating may be required.
   •  Add the methanol/sodium hydroxide solution to the soybean oil. Add a magnetic stirring
       bar and place on the hot plate. Heat to 60-65ºC and agitate for 1 hour. Pour into a
       separatory funnel and allow to separate.
Does separation occur?
What happens when you add water?
