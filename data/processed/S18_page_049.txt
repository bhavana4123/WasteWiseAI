Because we have assumed a fed-batch fermentation, the working volume varies over time, along
with the OTR and aeration rate in vvm (standard volume of air per volume of liquid per minute),
which are volumetric quantities. In absolute terms, the average aeration rate for a VG of 200
cm/min was 0.83 standard m3/s (including two days of offline time). The average impeller power
P was determined to be 260 kW (350 hp), with a maximum of 600 kW (800 hp). The final
specifications for the cellulase production bioreactors are shown in Table 19.

Four trains of three seed fermentors provide inoculum to the production bioreactors. Each vessel
in the seed trains is run batchwise on the same substrate as the production vessels. Air is also
sparged through each of the seed vessels, which are cooled with chilled water. The seed
bioreactors are each sized at 10% of the next bioreactor volume, i.e., 0.3 m3, 3 m3, and 30 m3.
The aeration demand is assumed to be 10% of the production aeration rate. Four trains were
chosen because each production fermentor has a total cycle time of 7 days, while each seed
fermentor should have a cycle time of 2 days (including cleaning and sterilization) to get through
the cell growth phase only.

Like the oxygen uptake rate, the glucose demand is also computed stoichiometrically from the
required protein production rate. Ammonia and SO2 are fed to the reactors stoichiometrically and
CSL, trace nutrients, and antifoam (corn oil) are added to the substrate based on flow rate. The
required nutrient concentrations are taken from Schell et al. [58] and are restated in Table 20.
